# Question Patterns

Load with `knowledge-retention-interviewing` when designing topics/questions (`GenerateTopicsAndQuestions`). Adapt the *slot*; never paste example wording. Every live question must name something from this interviewee's discovery.

## Pattern anatomy

Name (a) a specific thing from discovery, (b) a concrete situation, (c) lived experience:

- Weak: "What are the challenges in your role?"
- Strong: "When the monthly SAP batch run fails on a weekend, walk me through what you do before calling IT."

## Knowledge slots

Fill across the topics (one topic may cover more than one slot). Prefer the strong shape.

| Slot | Ask for | Strong shape |
|---|---|---|
| Failure / intervention | Last time the named process broke outside the manual | "Walk me through the last time [named process]…" |
| Single-person / hidden assets | Scripts, configs, informal ownership | "What in [named system] only you maintain, and where does it live?" |
| Stakeholders / escalation | Real decision path vs org chart | "Who do you actually call when [named situation], in what order?" |
| Judgment / exceptions | Thresholds and when to break process | "How do you decide when to [named decision] vs escalate?" |
| Successor landmines | Week-one surprises, fragile rituals | "What would go wrong in month one if nobody told your successor?" |

## Worked example (adapt names only)

Fake discovery: role = Production Planner; tools = SAP PP, Excel handover sheet; focus = weekend batch failures and vendor escalations.

- Failure: "When the monthly SAP PP batch run fails on a weekend, walk me through what you do before calling IT."
- Hidden assets: "Which Excel macros or local files for the handover sheet only you maintain, and where do they live?"
- Stakeholders: "Who do you actually call when a vendor misses a delivery window, in what order?"
- Judgment: "How do you decide when to resequence an order yourself vs escalate to the shift lead?"
- Successor: "What would go wrong in month one if nobody told your successor about the weekend batch workaround?"

## Universal successor topic (optional last topic)

Still substitute their real names/systems; do not paste unchanged.

- "What took you longest to learn that you wish someone had told you in week one?"
- "Which recurring meeting, report, or task looks unimportant but isn't? Why?"
- "Who are the three people your successor must build a relationship with first, and why each?"
- "What's the one thing you're worried will go wrong after you leave?"
